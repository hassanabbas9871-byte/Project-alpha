# The Ninth Bell: campaign notes

This document contains story spoilers. VEILBOUND uses an original setting,
cast, and mystery. Its influences are the atmosphere of occult detective
fiction: rainy streets, dangerous rituals, masks, and institutions that know
more than they admit. It does not adapt another novel's characters or plot.

Harrowmere sells yesterday to pay for tomorrow. A licensed memory-broker can
remove a bad afternoon, a childhood fear, or the name of someone who died.
Most citizens believe that a memory vanishes when sold. Most citizens are
wrong.

You are Rowan Vale, the newest field investigator in the municipal Bureau of
Unusual Affairs. Your sister Mira has disappeared. Her death certificate is
already on your desk, but the spaces for her name, age, and cause of death
are blank. A brass funeral bell is missing from the city's sealed inventory.
Every time a bell rings after midnight, someone else forgets she existed.

Dr Iona Reed, a physician who treats memories as wounds, and Silas Venn, a
former stage magician who knows which masks are alive, become Rowan's
partners. Their strengths are different, and tactical equipment changes how
they work together. Cases mix combat with evidence, short story scenes, and
decisions between protecting people and obtaining quicker material rewards.

## Eight chapters

1. **Rain Ward.** A black envelope, a haunted tenement, a falsified ledger,
   and a funeral without bells lead to the Mourning Beadle. Mira was not the
   first person erased, and a city official signed for the missing bell.
2. **Brass Market.** Memory brokers trade childhoods as collateral. Follow
   the pawn tickets through a counterfeit mint and a clandestine auction.
   The Grin Collector reveals that Mira bought evidence with her own past.
3. **Salt Docks.** A ship delivers cargo to people who died in a flood years
   ago. A lighthouse transmits a coded message in Mira's voice. The Drowned
   Admiral guards the route to a hospital outside ordinary time.
4. **Mercy Hospital.** Patients are being given memories that are not theirs.
   A compassionate experiment became a means of producing obedient witnesses.
   The Suture Saint protects the operating theatre and the names of its donors.
5. **Painted Mile.** An abandoned theatrical district stages real identities.
   Silas must face the troupe he fled. Behind the Maestro of Borrowed Faces,
   the party learns that Mira entered the conspiracy deliberately.
6. **Archive Below.** Bureau records describe the flood differently from the
   survivors. Harrowmere's charter is a bargain, its missing citizens the
   interest. Registrar Null edits the living as easily as a clerk edits paper.
7. **Bell Foundry.** The nine bells contain the city's accumulated memories.
   Workers are forced to cast a new witness for an old contract. Defeat the
   Iron Cantor and recover Mira before the final bell is tuned.
8. **Pale Conservatory.** Forgotten lives wait in a greenhouse without a sun.
   The last investigator to seek the truth became the Vicar of the Hollow
   Hour. End the collection, recover the witnesses, and decide what Harrowmere
   is allowed to remember.

Each case has two tactical encounters, for **64 campaign encounters**. The
last case of each chapter ends with a boss. Objectives vary between defeating
hostiles, sealing ritual sites, holding out under pressure, and reaching an
extraction point. The same case can play differently as the party's weapons,
skills, and equipment change.

## Length and pacing

The campaign is designed for at least two hours of thoughtful play through
64 encounters, story scenes, and loadout decisions. This is a design target,
not a stopwatch guarantee: reading speed, difficulty, and tactical familiarity
affect completion time. No artificial waiting, real-time gates, or repeated
grinding is required to advance the story. A human campaign playthrough is
needed to establish a measured completion time.

The game is intentionally compact enough to study as a C++ project. Battles
have finite objectives, rewards arrive after cases, and defeat allows a retry
instead of deleting a campaign. Saving lets a long campaign be played across
several sessions.
